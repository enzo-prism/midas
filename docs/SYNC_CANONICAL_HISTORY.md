# Canonical numeric history

Tailscale sync retains winning event identities and provider account-day reports in `MidasUsageCanonicalStore`. Canonical source history, the app's durable upload outbox, received publication cache, and transport page cache use separate roots. Scanner caches and source-log availability never authorize deletion from canonical history. Canonical retention does not expire measurements after 365 days; the existing display window and legacy compatibility publication remain bounded separately.

The store writes owner-only immutable SHA-256 pages, immutable publication manifests, then an atomic fsynced head. An interrupted write leaves the previous head usable. Callers may pin the returned manifest revision in a separate atomic checkpoint and load that exact revision after restart. Call `prune(retainingRevisions:)` only after the owning checkpoint commits, supplying every manifest it references. Pruning retains those manifests and each current/previous head and deletes only unreferenced encodings. It does not delete identities from the winning publication. Corruption or unsafe files fail closed; originals and previously committed files are not rewritten in place.

Each canonical publication and the combined display ledger permit at most two million rows. Disk pages have a one MiB guard and hold at most 512 events plus 512 account rows. Public publication APIs still materialize a bounded array in memory; paging removes the legacy single 32 MiB serialization limit, rather than making all scans constant-memory. Exceeding the canonical safety limit aborts collection/retention instead of committing incomplete history.

# Selection and legacy migration

A stable provider event identity selects its whole winning measurement: greatest token count, latest original observation time, greatest pricing revision, most complete pricing metadata, then deterministic serialization. An authoritative account-day report selects its latest observation first, permitting corrections downward. A later copied streaming prefix cannot replace a completed event. Source files are not modified, and copies retain their original identities even after derived rollups are produced.

Legacy rollups have already erased their constituent identities. Migration preserves each opaque aggregate alongside discovered fine events. The display never adds the fine rows to that same device/provider/day aggregate. Fine evidence replaces its displayed baseline only when all known token, request and monetary totals cover that baseline. A legacy-coverage warning remains because matching or exceeding totals cannot prove constituent membership. A fully rehydrated canonical day can receive disjoint late events and deduplicate copied logs exactly. Migration cannot retrospectively prove those properties for an opaque aggregate without its original measurements.

# Pricing revisions

`MidasUsageSyncValues.pricingRevision` is optional; absent legacy values mean generation zero. Exported priced event values carry `MidasUsageSyncExporter.pricingCatalogGeneration`, initially 1. Bump that controlled constant whenever changing the bundled/controlled pricing catalog or pricing policy, and add an equal-observation regression that allows the revised cost to decrease. Do not derive the revision from scan time, delivery time, or the app build number. Unknown prices remain unknown, and original event identities do not change. This field gives future catalog generations an explicit order; it cannot retrospectively order two already-priced legacy values that both lack a revision. Dynamically refreshed upstream prices within one controlled generation retain deterministic selection and require an intentional generation bump to establish correction precedence.
